New to D&D? Start here

Everything you need for your first night, in about five minutes.

How the game works

  1. The DM describes the world. "The tavern is warm and loud. A notice board by the door is covered in papers."
  2. You say what your character does. Anything at all: talk, sneak, climb, ask a question, eat the suspicious pie.
  3. If it's risky, you roll. Roll the d20 (the big 20-sided die), add the number from your sheet. High is good. The DM tells you what happens.

Roll some dice

Tap a die to roll it.

 

d20: almost every check and attack. The small dice: damage and healing.

The words you'll hear

Ability check, attack, saving throw

Ability check: trying something risky (climb, persuade, notice). Attack: trying to hit something. Saving throw: trying to avoid something bad happening to you (a trap, a spell). All three: roll d20 and add your bonus.

Advantage and disadvantage

Advantage: roll two d20s and keep the higher. Disadvantage: roll two and keep the lower. You get advantage when things go your way (an ally helps, the enemy can't see you).

Your turn in a fight

Fights go in turns, in order of Initiative (everyone rolls a d20 at the start). On your turn you can move (usually 30 feet), take one action (attack, cast a spell, help a friend, dash, hide), and maybe a bonus action if your class has one. You can also talk for free.

Armor Class (AC) and Hit Points (HP)

AC is how hard you are to hit: an enemy's attack roll has to meet or beat it. HP is how much damage you can take. At 0 HP you fall unconscious and start rolling death saves. Don't panic, your friends can help you up.

Death saves

At 0 HP, roll a d20 on your turn. 10 or higher is a success, under 10 is a failure. Three successes and you're stable. Three failures and things get serious. A natural 20 brings you back with 1 HP. Any healing gets you back up.

Short and long rests

Short rest: an hour of breathing and bandages, spend Hit Dice to heal. Long rest: a full night of sleep, get all your HP and most powers back.
